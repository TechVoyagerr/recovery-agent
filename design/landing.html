The problem

Indian merchants lose a quarter of every checkout, and then do nothing about it.

Roughly 25% of Indian online payments fail, OTP screens that time out, banks that go down at 9pm, UPI apps that throw errors, cards the issuer declines for no stated reason. The customer wanted to pay. The merchant wanted the money. The rails got in the way.

Today that failure hits a webhook, lands in a log, and dies there. Some merchants fire a generic "complete your payment" email six hours later. Most send nothing at all. Every one of those failures is a customer who had already decided to buy.

How it works

Four steps, and a fifth that makes the other four better.

Every failure goes through the same loop. The agent explains each decision in plain English, so a merchant can audit it without reading a model card.

  1. Step 01

    Detect

    A payment gateway payment.failed webhook lands. The agent holds the order open, resolves the customer, and starts a recovery within 240 ms. Abandoned carts are caught the same way.

  2. Step 02

    Diagnose

    The raw gateway error is classified into one of ten human reasons, OTP timeout, bank downtime, insufficient funds, with a certainty score. A card decline and a dropped network are not the same problem.

  3. Step 03

    Decide

    Strategy, channel, timing and message, chosen per failure, with a confidence score and a sentence of reasoning you can read. Insufficient funds waits for salary day. An OTP timeout gets 90 seconds.

  4. Step 04

    Act

    It creates a real payment link with the cart attached, sends the personalised nudge on WhatsApp, SMS or email, and schedules a smart retry, all inside your guardrails.

Step 05 · the compounding one

Learn

Every outcome is scored against the reason, channel and timing that produced it. The agent keeps a live recovery-rate matrix and rewrites its own playbook, so week four is materially better than week one, without anyone touching a rule.

Built on the payment gateway

Nothing bolted on. Four payment gateway APIs, used the way they were meant to be.

Payments API

Reads the failed payment, its method, its error code and the customer behind it. This is the raw material the diagnosis runs on.

GET /v1/payments/:id · GET /v1/orders/:id/payments

Payment Links API

Every recovery is a real, short-lived Payment Link: the original amount, the saved cart, a 48-hour expiry, and a reference back to the failed attempt.

POST /v1/payment_links · GET /v1/payment_links/:id

Webhooks

The whole loop is webhook-driven, signature-verified and idempotent, detection on the way in, outcome scoring on the way back.

payment.failed · payment.captured · payment_link.paid · order.paid

Subscriptions API

Failed mandates get the same treatment as one-off payments, the agent runs dunning on subscription.charged failures instead of letting churn happen silently.

GET /v1/subscriptions/:id · subscription.pending

FAQ

The questions a merchant asks first.

Will this spam my customers?

No, the guardrails exist for exactly this. Two nudges per customer per 24 hours, hard quiet hours, no chasing anything under ₹99, and an automatic stop after a deliberate cancel. The agent's own learning shows a third nudge loses more customers than it recovers, so it will not send one.

Do I have to let it act on its own?

Three autonomy levels. Suggest only drafts everything and sends nothing. Auto with approval acts below ₹5,000 and queues the rest for you. Fully autonomous runs the whole loop. You can change it at any time, and every action stays in an auditable timeline either way.

How is this different from an abandoned-cart email tool?

A cart tool sends one template on one schedule to everyone. This agent reads why the payment failed and picks a different play for each reason, hold and wait for a bank outage, 90 seconds for an OTP timeout, salary day for insufficient funds, offer UPI after an issuer decline. Then it scores what worked and changes its own behaviour.

Can I see why it made a particular decision?

Every recovery opens into a timeline: the raw failure, the diagnosis and its certainty, the decision with a confidence score and a plain-English reason, the exact payment link created, the exact message sent, and the outcome. Nothing is a black box you have to trust.

What does it cost me if a recovery fails?

One message and an expired link. The order was already lost, the agent's downside is a single WhatsApp template, and it deliberately skips reasons where chasing is not worth it, like repeated deliberate cancels.

How long until it is better than my current rules?

It ships with a playbook derived from aggregate Indian failure patterns, so day one already beats a single generic email. From there it re-scores after every outcome; in the demo run the reason × channel matrix stabilised at around 400 events per cell.
